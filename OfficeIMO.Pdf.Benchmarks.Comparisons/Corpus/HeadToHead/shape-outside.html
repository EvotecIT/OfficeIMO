Shape outside

A circle floated to the left pulls the lines of text around its curve. Editors have used the effect for pull quotes and portraits for a long time, and the CSS shapes module brought it to the web. The text in this paragraph is long enough to run past the bottom of the circle and return to the full measure of the column, which shows that the wrap ends where the shape ends.

An ellipse floated to the right wraps text on its left side. Line lengths change gradually rather than in a step, and a careful engine will measure the intersection of the shape with each line box separately, so that the edge of the paragraph follows the outline smoothly from one line to the next. Further words carry the block past the end of the shape.

A polygon gives the text a diagonal edge. The first lines stay short, the following lines grow a little longer each time, and by the bottom of the float they nearly reach the original left margin. Polygons suit angular magazine layouts, where a photograph has been cut along a diagonal. More words follow so that the paragraph clearly fills the whole height of the polygon float and continues on below it.

Shape-margin adds breathing room around the shape, so the text stands a little further from the red circle than the plain float would give. The result reads as a soft halo of white space that keeps pace with the curve of the shape, as designers usually want.

A gradient can define a shape through its alpha channel with shape-image-threshold. Here the transparent half of the box lets the text run into the lower right part of the float, along a diagonal edge. The remaining words continue to the end of the shape and past it.
